A brushed rear-drive package has a useful purpose without an immediate conversion.

The 14-turn MEGA 550 motor supplies the rear-wheel-drive layout through the documented transmission and slipper arrangement. The current manufacturer specification identifies full ball bearings and a brushless-ready driveline. Full bearings are an actual equipment distinction; brushless-ready describes potential use, not the factory motor and controller. We do not turn either phrase into a measured efficiency, crash or reliability result. For this buyer, an understandable stock power path is part of the appeal.

Rear drive means the back tires supply acceleration while the front wheels steer without a driven front assembly. On loose ground, smooth throttle input is part of the learning project. That is a mechanical implication, not a personal handling observation. The included NiMH system lets the newcomer establish a baseline without separately researching every battery option. It is worth using before deciding that a different motor or controller is essential to enjoy the truck.

Keep the stock gear and wheel combination at first. The manufacturer advertises 20-plus mph for the package, but we have not measured it or promised that pace on every surface. Dense grass, binding wheels and repeated acceleration change motor load. If the truck spends most of the session dragging, find a better route instead of buying taller speed gearing. A conversion can be a rewarding separate project, but it should be priced and chosen deliberately rather than presented as a mandatory step for every new Gorgon owner.

04 /

A simple limiter can let a beginner focus on direction and stopping.

The SLT2 transmitter provides proportional steering and throttle with a documented output limiter. The fitted package is not a DSC or AVC system. Limiting available output addresses the pace of the first session; it does not provide automatic steering corrections. Read the current radio and controller instructions to understand neutral, braking, reverse and battery settings. A simple control system can be a benefit when the owner wants to begin driving without managing an application or several assistance modes.

Mark a broad practice route and a clear stopping area. Start with modest output, practice steering when the truck approaches you and learn the trigger's transition from driving to braking. Keep the factory battery while making that comparison. It is easier to identify progress when the setup stays consistent. If the truck pulls or fails to steer freely, inspect wheel and linkage condition before using trim to conceal a mechanical problem.

The 40A controller and receiver are combined. That can keep the stock package coordinated while affecting future radio or power changes. If you later choose a compatible 2S LiPo, use the documented battery-mode settings and proper charging route. Connector fit alone is insufficient. Our controls assessment rewards the accessible limiter and straightforward stock arrangement for a first rear-drive monster buyer, without claiming measured radio range, response time, braking performance or assistance that is not fitted.

05 /

Large tires make mechanical inspection part of the beginner routine.

The S660 digital servo, independent suspension and oil-filled shocks are documented factory equipment. Check that steering moves freely and returns to neutral before putting the truck onto a demanding surface. A big tire pressed against an obstacle can load the linkage without creating a useful turn. Avoid holding the steering against that resistance. The correct first response to binding is inspection of contact and damaged parts, not automatically a stronger servo or more trim.

The Gorgon's tall monster shape and 295mm documented wheelbase describe the activity you are choosing. They do not establish a rollover angle or a landing-control score. Begin with broad direction changes and modest bumps. If you later adjust suspension, record the supplied settings and compare one change on a repeated part of the route. More preload and different damping are separate decisions, so a stiffer-looking setup should not be accepted as universally better.

Greg Vogel's RC Driver RTR review discusses the stock truck's recreational driving and notes considerations around cooling, the battery tray and rear-drive use. Those are attributable independent observations, not our test. We do not carry its broad toughness praise into a measured rating. The useful editorial conclusion is that the documented steering and suspension give a newcomer a credible starting point, while maintenance and deliberate control remain part of using a real RC vehicle.

06 /

Monster tires give the truck a brief, not unlimited access.

The dBoots Chevron MT tires and tall stance fit ordinary outdoor monster-truck recreation. Firm dirt, moderate uneven ground and short grass are reasonable places to begin assessing a route. Check for deep holes, sharp debris and hard edges near corners. Leave space for stopping rather than creating a circuit that uses every meter for acceleration. A body that resembles a big aggressive truck is enjoyable, but the actual tire scale determines how substantial an ordinary obstacle becomes.

Dense or wet growth can create sustained drag and collect around moving components. Use short initial inspections while learning the site, and choose another patch if the truck is continually working against vegetation. Holding more throttle is not a useful substitute for choosing suitable ground. Rear-wheel drive also remains relevant on loose surfaces; the limiter can give you time to learn input timing without converting the vehicle into a four-wheel-drive traction system.

An independent video of a skate-park session is another publisher's use, not an operating prescription for every beginner. This review does not establish a maximum jump height, water depth, runtime or damage record. The current manufacturer documents the installed hardware and its maintenance conditions. Our recommendation stays with repeatable recreational ground where a new owner can see, steer and stop the truck, then expand the route based on actual observed control and mechanical condition.
