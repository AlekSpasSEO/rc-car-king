A finished vehicle still leaves a substantial rechargeable-equipment decision.

The current contents include the assembled truck, painted clipless body, Firma 4074 2050Kv motor, 150A Smart V2 ESC, SLT3 transmitter, SR315 receiver, S665 servo, aluminum servo horn, AA cells and tools. These are coordinated installed electronics rather than parts awaiting a build. The separate receiver is a meaningful ownership distinction from ARRMA's combined 223S units. It does not mean the stock package provides AVC assistance or every optional telemetry accessory.

Drive LiPo and charger are separate, with the manufacturer documenting 4S and 6S routes. Follow the exact battery arrangement and loop-wire instructions in the manual rather than improvising a connector combination. Verify the 158 × 48 × 70mm maximum tray and actual restraint. The required-equipment page also identifies a LiPo charging bag. Include it and any separate charger input power in the startup basket. You already receive radio AAs in this particular package.

Inspect the truck before its first powered session: wheels, steering, body latches, wire routing and battery retention. Set the radio and ESC according to the manual and use a conservative initial route. Even an experienced driver may need time to recalibrate expectations from a smaller vehicle. Start with normal steering, acceleration and braking checks, not an ambitious jump. A correctly established stock baseline is more useful than making several changes before the vehicle's ordinary behavior is familiar.

Before the first session

We identify the part and both published figures rather than inventing a measured torque value or silently choosing one. If that rating determines your purchase, ask the manufacturer about voltage and specification basis.

Check steering endpoints and free movement with the truck supported. Large tires can demand substantial steering effort when stationary or trapped against terrain. A stronger-looking horn does not make a mechanical stop harmless. Inspect the bellcranks, links and contact points before concluding a different servo is needed. The current drawings show the fitted assemblies, and exact revision-aware service is more useful than a generic list of modifications claimed necessary for every 6S Kraton.

For suspension changes, use a repeatable corner and modest uneven feature. Record factory settings, then change one item related to a repeated behavior. A setup aimed at large jumps can make another part of an ordinary mixed route less pleasant. More preload, different fluid and replacement springs do different jobs. Our equipment assessment values documented adjustment and a substantial factory specification without turning it into a damping, steering-force or landing-control result from an unperformed test.

The questions worth asking.

Is this the Kraton EXB?+

The reviewed complete SKU is ARA8608V6T1 standard V6. ARA8708V6 is a separate EXB trim; do not treat their equipment lists as interchangeable.

What chassis material is documented now?+

The current official standard-V6 page explicitly lists a black-anodized 7075-T6 chassis. Earlier coverage omitting a material specification is not substituted for current evidence.

Does it include AVC?+

The documented stock SLT3 / SR315 combination is not presented as an AVC package. Follow the actual radio and ESC instructions.

What rechargeable equipment is missing?+

A documented compatible 4S or 6S LiPo arrangement, suitable charger and its input power. The factory contents include transmitter AAs.

Why is the wheelbase note unusual?+

The web table labels 377mm as Wheel Width; the current manual identifies the wheelbase. We flag the label difference rather than invent a measurement.

Is 65-plus mph a result from this review?+

No. It is a manufacturer claim under stated battery and gearing conditions. Our score concerns the purchase for an experienced recreational basher.
