01 /

The V6 is the long Talion

Start with the space the truck occupies. The V6 is 581 mm long and 430 mm wide, with a 411 mm wheelbase under a low body. That combination points to a broad route with time to place the truck before each corner. It is a substantial change from the earlier 1/8 Talion that appears in used listings and older driving videos. If a listing simply says Talion EXB, make the seller show the model code before using this review to judge the package.

The exact truck is ARA7707V6T1. The body is mainly black with red graphics, and the 4074 motor and Firma V2 ESC are fitted. RC Driver’s April 2026 feature names the same SKU and shows the revised chassis and body. It can help you recognize the vehicle. The buyer judgments here come from the exact dimensions, electronics and assembly documents, with no measured driving claims.

The Talion overlaps a Kraton in price and power class, which makes the available route the better starting point. If a typical outing gives you a long firm lane, a visible corner and space to stop, the Talion’s layout earns consideration. If your area mostly offers short bursts between obstacles, the larger footprint can shorten the useful part of the drive.

02 /

Quote a 4S setup before you count on 6S

The box brings the assembled truck, SLT3 radio, four AAs and maintenance tools. The drive pack and balance charger are separate. ARRMA documents a compatible single hard-case 4S or 6S pack, or a matching pair of supported hard-case 2S or 3S packs. The supplied loop connector is for the unused input in the single-pack configuration shown in the manual. Use that exact wiring diagram, and keep connectors and leads secured away from the rotating driveline.

The source conflict is worth keeping in view. ARRMA’s online showcase lists 35C at 5000 mAh as its minimum. The exact manual specifies 50C at 5000 mAh minimum and recommends 100C at 5000 mAh. For the budget here, use the stricter manual and a pack meeting its recommended rating. Use the product support contact to resolve any package revision or compatibility uncertainty before buying batteries.

A conservative first quote is one supported 4S pack and the correct charger. Confirm physical tray fit with the case and lead exit, and verify the charging protocol for the exact pack. If you already own two batteries with the right cell count, compare their model, capacity, condition and compatibility before counting them as a usable pair.

03 /

A 2050Kv power system and a low, wide running gear

The power path starts at the 4074 Firma motor and 150A Smart V2 ESC. In this shaft-driven four-wheel layout, the center gear differential and the two axle differentials let the wheels and axles accommodate differences in travel. The all-metal gear internals and steel driveshafts are factory equipment. They are reasons to compare this model with a truck you would otherwise build up, while the condition of bearings, gears and wheel retention still depends on inspection and use.

The EXB plate and bracing matter when you look at how much of the chassis spans between the axles. ARRMA lists a 3 mm 7075-T6 aluminum plate, aluminum front and rear bulkheads and braces across the chassis. The different materials do different jobs. The plate and braces form the backbone, while the composite arms and side guards remain their own assemblies. If one end takes a hit, trace the alignment and adjacent mounts before ordering the visibly broken piece alone.

Use the stock gearing as the baseline for dirt. The technical pack shows a 15T pinion and the product table lists a 50T spur. The manufacturer supplies a larger 23T option for its documented high-speed setup. The bigger pinion changes load, so it is the wrong starting point when you are learning a mixed route or repeatedly accelerating out of tight turns.

04 /

Use 4S and the throttle limit to learn the braking lane

The SLT3 provides 50, 75 and 100 percent throttle-limit positions. That switch caps the transmitter’s command. It does not tell you the truck’s speed, shorten the line by itself, or set the battery mode. Begin with the supported 4S configuration, the factory gearing and the lowest limit. Set the truck up using the exact radio sequence and stop if steering, throttle or failsafe checks do not match the instructions.

Mark an easy entrance and exit on firm ground, then establish where you need to release throttle and apply brakes to stop in a repeatable space. Repeat the test from the same heading, because an oblique stop on loose dirt can create a different path. If the truck consistently stops wide of the lane, make the lane larger or move the braking mark earlier before trying a higher setting.

A 4S pack still drives a large truck. Leave a margin beyond the stopping point and keep people out of the driving area. Do not mix the throttle-limit experiment with a gearing change. The stock package gives you a useful baseline and enough range to discover whether your venue has room for this truck at all.

05 /

Steering rate and tire clearance do different jobs

The S665 metal-geared digital servo and SLT3 steering rate give you a solid starting point for a wide front end. In a long bend you may only need a small steering input, particularly on a consistent surface. A marked turn is a better test than swinging the transmitter wheel around on a bench. Begin with a broad arc and observe where the front tires point when the truck comes back onto a straight section.

If you see a new pull after a bump, use the opposite front corner as a comparison and inspect the linkage, wheel and hub with the pack disconnected. A tire that touches something under compression can steer the truck as it passes over a rut. Changing trim can make it appear straight on a smooth surface while leaving the rub in place. Check the whole sweep around the body and links before changing the radio setting.

The 17 mm wheels are only one part of the stock geometry. The belted Katar tires have a published outside diameter of 145 mm. A replacement with the same hub label can move the shoulders or change the rolling diameter. Use the technical pack to check the assembly and compare dimensions before replacing one side or moving to a different tire.

06 /

Favor broad, firm routes with a clear exit

The low body and long wheelbase suit a relatively flat surface with enough room to set up a turn. Walk a proposed line and pay particular attention to abrupt crests and ruts that cross it. The wheels can be on sound ground on either side of a crest while the center of the chassis catches. A wide path around a break in the surface gives this truck more to do than a narrow lane that forces it over every obstacle.

Use packed dirt or short, even grass where the truck can turn and brake without crossing into a shared path. Taller grass and loose soil can increase load. Watch the vehicle’s behavior rather than assume a cooling fan compensates for continual drag. Follow the manual’s cooling and fault guidance between runs. If you can see the tires working harder in one section, use a clear section to separate terrain drag from a fault in the driveline.

For a gentle rise, look at the landing and the stopping area before the approach. A long truck needs a longer clean landing area to settle than the obstacle height suggests. If you only have a small yard, price a smaller complete truck before assuming the big Talion gives you more use.

07 /

Open the body and separate wear from a fault

The stock shell and wing come fitted, and the battery is accessed with the body removed. The retained clips are useful around dirt because they stay with the body during access. Keep the body off when inspecting the battery leads, gear area and motor fan, with the power disconnected. Check the fan opening for debris and confirm the tray straps still hold the pack the way the manual shows.

After a run, start at the wheel assemblies. A belted tire still needs inspection for tread damage, glue lifting or a wheel that wobbles as you turn it by hand. The rear and front driveshaft construction differs, so use the correct end of the diagram when you are looking for a boot, joint or outdrive. If one axle suddenly makes a noise, work inward from the wheel to the shaft and differential before assuming a center fault.

If you need to carry the truck to your driving area, use the published length and width to check the bag or box with room for the rear wing. ARRMA pairs 11.64 lb with 5.2 kg. Converting the pound value gives about 5.28 kg, so use a scale if you need to know the ready-to-drive weight with your pack. The listing does not identify the weighing setup. The model’s weight in the hand is one more reason to handle it by the chassis when you transport it.

The EXB parts list is useful after an impact because related hardware can carry different numbers from another 6S model. Use ARA7707V6 as the family in the technical pack and match the actual location on the truck. That is a better repair habit than ordering parts by the 6S badge or assuming the earlier 1/8 Talion drawings still apply.

09 /

Three complete trucks that solve a different venue problem

The Kraton 6S BLX V6 is the direct alternative if you want a big open-ground truck and prefer the more general-purpose speed-truck brief. Compare the actual chassis, wheels and package price from the exact review, and carry the battery and charger line into both totals. Pick the Talion when the longer, lower layout is the equipment you want to drive.

The Mojave 4S makes more sense if you want a desert-truck shell over the wheels and your site rewards a truck with a different profile and less demanding power budget. Consider how you will get to the battery and what happens when the covered wheels pick up dirt. Compare the exact Mojave package and chassis measurements instead of assuming its power system swaps with the Talion.

The Mini Kraton 3S is the alternative when storage and driving space set the budget. It gives up the big Talion’s footprint, which is the advantage on a smaller course. Measure the tightest section you use regularly. If the big truck cannot turn and stop there comfortably, a smaller truck is the one you will be able to drive for the whole session.

10 /

Make the venue part of the purchase

The Talion V6 earns its price for the experienced driver who wants the actual EXB equipment and has broad open ground. The longer chassis, low shell, 2050Kv system and belted tires form a clear package. There is no need to open the optional gearing bag to get a useful first session. The supported 4S plan and transmitter limiter are enough to start working out braking distance and course fit.

The main drawback is how quickly you run out of usable space when the terrain is tight. For a driver without a wide, controlled site, the wheelbase and width are costs you carry to every outing. Battery and charger costs also make this a four-figure purchase from an empty bench on the stated assumptions. Run those numbers before comparing vehicle-only prices.

Check the listing code and the power equipment against the exact manual. Then sketch the route you can use most often, including where you would stop. If you have a meaningful line to drive on firm ground and want a large truggy with those factory parts, the V6 is a sensible shortlist.

BEFORE YOU ORDER /

The questions worth asking.

Is this the older 1/8 Talion?+

No. ARA7707V6T1 is the larger 1/7 Talion 6S EXB V6 with a 411 mm wheelbase.

Does the Talion come with a battery or charger?+

The box includes the transmitter and four AAs. The drive pack and balance charger are separate.

Can I start on 4S?+

Yes. Use the manufacturer’s documented 4S configuration, pack limits and wiring. The example uses a supported single 4S hard-case pack.

Why is the published battery minimum different in two places?+

The showcase lists 35C at 5000 mAh. The exact manual gives 50C at 5000 mAh minimum and recommends 100C at 5000 mAh. Use the stricter manual requirement and confirm compatibility.

Is the larger pinion needed for ordinary dirt driving?+

Use the stock gearing for the baseline. The optional 23T gear is part of the manufacturer’s separate speed configuration.

Where is the Talion a good fit?+

Broad, dry, firm dirt or another clear off-road site with controlled access and room to stop.
