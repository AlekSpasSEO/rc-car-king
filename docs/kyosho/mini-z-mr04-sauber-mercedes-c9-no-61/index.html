01 /

Buy the No. 61 Readyset by SKU

Choose the 32362KR if the blue Sauber-Mercedes is the body you want to put on the grid. It is the complete No. 61 car with Kyosho’s 102 mm wide LM layout, assembled chassis and matched handheld radio. A seller showing a C9 shell on its own has not answered the same buying question. Ask for the box identifier, especially when photos show only the livery or the wording says little about the chassis.

Kyosho names the model LM1988 in the product title. Its descriptive image text instead mentions the 1987 race. The organizer’s records distinguish the years and the entries, and Endurance-Info identifies the full-size No. 61 C9 in the 1988 Sauber-Mercedes entry. For the RC purchase, 32362KR and the photographed blue No. 61 body are the reliable identifiers. The inconsistent caption makes a year-only comparison with another Kyosho C9 a poor way to order.

The other reason to slow down before checkout is the club rulebook. The model has enough pieces in the box to drive at home once cells are installed. A local class may use a different definition of stock, with rules for rear pods, tires, gears or the body itself. Send the organizer the SKU, wheelbase and pod designation before buying if racing is the reason for choosing this one.

02 /

The car and radio arrive together

Kyosho includes the finished No. 61 body, assembled chassis, KT-531P handset, wheels and tires, front tread adjustment pieces, wheel nuts and basic tools. The pinion options are 6T through 9T. Put the supplied loose parts into a labeled bag rather than treating them as universal spares for every Mini-Z. The pinion tool and nut wrench make it easier to restore the stock car after an adjustment.

Use eight alkaline AAA cells for the straightforward startup example. The exact manufacturer page gives contradictory rechargeable-cell wording across two requirements blocks. Alkaline cells are the common documented option for both compartments. If you plan to use rechargeable cells, consult the instructions for your exact chassis and radio before buying them. A battery that fits the tray physically still needs to meet the instructions for the device it is powering.

Check that the body is seated before placing the car on a course. A low prototype nose and a long rear deck put visible bodywork close to the barriers. Start with the wheels and tires that came fitted. A new tire diameter or offset can change clearance, and those changes are easier to recognize when you have already seen how the original package sits.

03 /

The long LM pod determines several later choices

The 130-class brushed motor drives the rear wheels through a geared differential. Kyosho fits the axles with ball bearings and supports the rear pod on a T-bar with a spring damper. That compact assembly is why parts choice starts with the pod designation. An MR-04 listing can describe several wheelbases and rear assemblies, while this one uses MR-04W-LM at 102 mm.

The differential lets the rear wheels rotate at different rates as the car turns. The rear suspension lets the pod move relative to the chassis. After a barrier touch, either a rubbing tire or a pod that moves less freely can make the car feel different. With the cells removed, check tire and body clearance and compare free movement before treating the stock differential as the first thing to replace.

Keep the installed gearing until you have a reason to change it. The included pinions give documented options, but the track and class can decide which one is useful or permitted. If a venue restricts the gear, confirm the mesh instructions and check for smooth movement by hand after any change. Write down what was installed before opening the gear cover so you can get back to the same baseline.

04 /

Learn steering rate with the stock wheels in place

The matched KT-531P provides the initial controls, including trim and steering dual rate. Kyosho also describes a reduced-speed training mode. Start there if you are learning the corner spacing and use the exact handset instructions to check the active mode. A long prototype body can look slow from above until it is already close to the next board.

Trim recenters the controls and dual rate changes the amount of steering commanded. If the tire contacts the body with the steering turned, look at the wheel installation and body fit before deciding that more steering will help. A repeatable low-speed turn on a clear track is a useful way to decide whether the current steering travel is enough for the course.

Kyosho lists the MZW711 gyro as optional. Check the class rules before planning to install it, and follow the actual chassis documentation if the venue allows it. For a new Readyset owner, the stock radio, a clear course and known tire fit are enough to learn where the car needs room. Adding another setup variable makes it harder to tell why the first corner improved.

05 /

Write down the unusual front and rear offsets

The C9’s stock wheels are narrow +2.7 mm fronts and wide +0.8 mm rears. Kyosho lists tread at 68.5 mm front and 66.0 mm rear and a 78.0 mm overall width. Tread and overall width answer different questions. Tread describes where the wheels sit relative to each other, while the body can still be the part that has to clear a course barrier or class gauge.

When checking a replacement wheel, compare width and offset together. A pair that reaches a similar outside width may still leave the inner edge near the chassis or the outer edge near the fender under steering. Check the fit with the car settled and the body clipped into place. Spin the wheels and look for rub before you commit to driving at higher steering rates.

Kyosho includes front tread adjustment pieces to support other body configurations. A shell swap can also require a different rear assembly and differential hardware. Use the manufacturer’s exact compatibility list and the body’s fit information before buying anything to convert the Sauber. If the reason for buying is the blue No. 61, keeping its wheels and pod labeled will make a later restoration straightforward.

Record the offset from the wheel packaging or the manufacturer data rather than estimating it from the livery photo. The front tire’s relationship to the arch changes as the steering sweeps. A wheel that looks flush when the car is parked can still foul at an angle. Hold the original wheels aside when trying an eligible set, so you can restore the 32362KR if you need to isolate a clearance problem.

06 /

Compare the box against the actual class rules

Houston RC posts separate Mini-Z class definitions with specific chassis, body and equipment requirements. Use that as an example of how much the wording can vary between classes even at one venue. Ask your own organizer about MR-04W-LM, 102 mm and the exact shell, then ask which tires and pinion are permitted. A broad statement about Mini-Z compatibility is less helpful than an answer tied to the class you will enter.

For a home layout, start with a dry, clean indoor surface that does not catch the low nose. The C9 measures 181 mm long and the manufacturer lists 40 mm overall height. Lay out a sweeping return and put the end of the straight where you have time to slow down. Take a few easy passes and watch the nose and rear wing relative to the barriers before moving any markers inward.

A dusty floor and a prepared miniature circuit can make the same stock setup feel quite different. Keep the tire configuration constant while comparing steering adjustments. If the car becomes inconsistent after crossing a seam or collecting fibers, clear the course and inspect the wheels before you tune. The value of this Readyset is being able to repeat a small indoor route, and a consistent surface makes those repetitions count.

For a first visit, bring the stock parts and your class notes rather than opening the car on race day to install a different pod. If the organizer permits the long car but wants specific tires, ask how they check width with those tires fitted. Recheck under the shell before joining the grid. That is where the documented 78 mm overall width and the club’s own measuring procedure become separate facts.

07 /

Keep the finished C9 body in its intended configuration

The No. 61 body comes painted and marked. The manufacturer explicitly says the optional MZW429R light unit cannot be installed on this shell. If a lighting project is central to the purchase, confirm a compatible complete model instead of assuming a general Mini-Z accessory will fit. This body’s identity is the main reason to buy it and deserves its own check before you choose accessories.

Use the body removal procedure in the manual and avoid lifting the car by the wing. Look at the front mount and wheel openings after a strike. A slight shift in the shell can show up as a steering symptom because the wheel reaches the edge on only one side. If a replacement shell becomes necessary, order by its own part number and distinguish it from the complete 32362KR Readyset.

The manufacturer’s technical photos include views of the broader MR-04 platform. Use them to recognize the mechanisms, then use the exact C9 parts and compatibility listings to order. Keep a setup card with the stock offsets, the installed gearing and the 102 mm configuration. That is particularly helpful if several similar Readysets share a tool case.

Check the wheel offsets and body dimensions even though the wheelbase matches. If both suit the class, the choice can come down to the silhouette and which exact configuration you prefer to maintain.

The MINI-Z MR-04 Porsche 911 GT3 RS is a shorter complete road-car option with its own rear-motor layout. It is worth considering if a tighter indoor course or a class limit prompted the comparison. Confirm the Porsche’s specific geometry rather than using its smaller body as proof it will pass the local rule sheet.

The AWD Initial D Toyota AE86 addresses a different indoor driving task. It gives you driven front wheels and a package intended for drifting. Choose it when sliding through a small layout is the point of the session. The C9 is the option here for a long rear-drive scale racer and a suitable grip course.

The questions worth asking.

Is 32362KR a complete car or just the shell?+

It is the complete Readyset with assembled chassis, finished body and KT-531P radio. The AAA cells are separate.

What wheelbase does the No. 61 Readyset use?+

The exact product page gives 102.0 mm, marked 3L, with the MR-04W-LM chassis.

Can it race in a box stock class?+

Ask the organizer whether that class admits the 102 mm wheelbase, LM rear pod and exact shell. Check its tire and gearing rules at the same time.
