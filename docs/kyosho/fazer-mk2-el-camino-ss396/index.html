Drivetrain and power: Fazer Mk2 1969 Chevy El Camino SS396 Cortez Silver

The FZ02L uses shaft-driven full-time 4WD and front/rear gear differentials. The G14L is a brushed motor, and the manufacturer lists a 6.54:1 stock ratio. This is a conventional road-car driveline that shares broad hardware concepts with other Fazer Mk2 cars. It is not rear-wheel drive simply because the full-size El Camino was associated with that layout. Keep the RC drivetrain separate from the historical vehicle’s specification.

The differentials allow left and right wheels to accommodate different corner distances. Sealed gear housings reduce direct contamination paths but do not make the entire car dustproof. Check that the wheels turn freely and that debris is not building around exposed drive joints after a session. A tight joint or damaged wheel attachment should be resolved mechanically, not masked by selecting a more powerful motor.

The platform’s fixed-position motor mounting accommodates specified pinions, so later gearing changes have a documented process. The stock arrangement is the appropriate initial baseline. Replacing the brushed system with brushless power can be an enjoyable separate project, but its cost and setup would change the recommendation. I would purchase this Readyset when the finished El Camino and its supplied road package are desirable together. If a conversion is already mandatory, compare the total with a complete vehicle sold in that configuration before using the car-only price to declare the silver model a bargain.

Radio and setup: Fazer Mk2 1969 Chevy El Camino SS396 Cortez Silver

The supplied KT-231P+ and installed servo mean you do not need to solve a radio integration project merely to drive the car. Check neutral trim, steering direction, braking and the controller’s startup procedure before placing it on open ground. A familiar transmitter shape is not evidence that every setting matches another Kyosho you have used. Read the actual instructions and keep the first drive straightforward.

Set steering travel so the servo does not continue to push at a stop. With the body installed, confirm that the tires can turn without rubbing. If the car tracks incorrectly, look at the mechanical alignment and tire condition as well as radio trim. Keep battery and electronics leads clear of the center shaft and steering linkage after each pack change. Those practical checks matter even when the exterior arrives beautifully finished.

Steering and suspension: Fazer Mk2 1969 Chevy El Camino SS396 Cortez Silver

The stock package combines independent suspension with oil-filled shocks and a fitted KS202W steering servo. Those are meaningful road-car components, already installed. The long FZ02L wheelbase is 274 mm, compared with shorter FZ02 shells elsewhere in the lineup. We report the configuration as a purchase distinction, without claiming that the longer car has been measured as more stable or slower to change direction.

For the first setup, inspect shock attachment and free movement, compare both sides and check that the wheels are properly seated. The body’s wheel arches need to clear the actual stock wheel offset through steering and suspension travel. An alternative wheel that looks more realistic can move the tire into the body. Choose replacements by fit as well as appearance, and check the manufacturer’s offset requirements rather than assuming every touring rim is interchangeable.

If you adjust suspension later, preserve the original settings and use a repeatable practice route. Do not change tires, shocks and gearing together and then assign the whole result to one component. The decorative brake-disc hubs are scale styling rather than working wheel brakes. Our rating credits understandable documented hardware and accessible service. It does not measure steering response, suspension absorption or the effects of a hypothetical lowered stance. For a vintage-road buyer, a functioning stock baseline is a more useful starting point than tuning to a look before learning the car.

Where to drive: Fazer Mk2 1969 Chevy El Camino SS396 Cortez Silver

The El Camino belongs on smooth pavement and suitable on-road areas. Its pickup body does not make it a trail truck or monster truck. Vintage Touring Car tires and a low touring platform support a scale street-driving role. Walk the route and check for raised curbs, large cracks and stones before the first run; small RC wheels experience those features very differently from the real vehicle the body depicts.

Begin with controlled starts, stops and broad turns. Keep enough room around the long model to recover a poorly placed corner. The stock brushed package can still reach an obstacle quickly, so do not let the absence of a brushless label replace deliberate driving. We have not measured top speed or braking distance. Establish those practical limits through your own careful first sessions rather than attempting to reproduce footage of another model or electronics build.

After a dusty run, inspect exposed joints, shock shafts, wheel rotation and the battery area. The sealed transmissions help protect internal gears but do not eliminate external maintenance. Clean the body gently and check its mounts before storage. The servo’s waterproof designation is not a blanket guarantee for every connector, bearing and completed vehicle component. Follow the actual instructions for water and care. For the intended owner, dry scale-road use is the clearest way to enjoy the silver body while preserving its details and making ordinary chassis inspection manageable.

Buying verdict: Fazer Mk2 1969 Chevy El Camino SS396 Cortez Silver

The Cortez Silver El Camino is compelling when the exact pickup subject and an assembled road-driving package are the priorities. Fitted radio, brushed power, bearings and oil dampers create a complete standard car, while the finished body supplies the visual reward immediately. The cautions are the low-ground brief, separate power purchases and the manufacturer’s discontinued designation. Those are significant ownership details, not reasons to erase the appeal of the subject.

Our five categories assess complete-car preparation, scale presentation, documented running gear, service understanding and purchase value for a classic-pickup pavement driver. We do not score speed, grip, impact survival or reliability. The black T1 unboxing is labeled as sibling package context; it does not supply a measured result for the silver T2. Current manufacturer documents and exact API identity establish the reviewed variant independently of that older coverage.

I would take 34419T2 when it is specifically the El Camino you want, the stock brushed system fits your plans and a sensible source of parts and power equipment is available. Confirm the current offer, learn the stock controls and protect the shell while accessing the battery. I would pass for rough trails, frequent jumps or an immediate conversion that makes the supplied electronics redundant. Bought for smooth-ground scale driving, the long silver model has a clear role and a practical ownership case, provided the body style does not disguise the touring chassis underneath.
