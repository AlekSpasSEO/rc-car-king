01 /

Pick the Jaguar for its exact layout

This is the pink No.4 Le Mans 1991 Jaguar, sold as a complete Readyset under 32369LE. The broad prototype body is paired with a wide MR-04W-LM chassis and 102 mm wheelbase. That combination makes the review more specific than a generic introduction to the MR-04 platform. It determines how the wheels sit in the arches, whether the car meets your club rules and what you need to check if you plan to reuse another body.

Kyosho lists other versions in the XJR-14 range, including the No.3 Monza Readyset, and sells bodies separately. The color and race number alone do not tell you whether the box includes a chassis and transmitter. The historical affiliate identity names the No.4 32369LE Readyset. Check that exact code when following a comparison or reading an offer from another market.

For a first MINI-Z, the package is appealing if the goal is a finished scale car for a clean circuit. A buyer who already owns a shorter chassis should think of this as a second configuration with its own notes. The longer wheelbase and particular tire placement are reasons to ask about the venue and class before the body makes the purchase decision for you.

02 /

The Readyset comes assembled and needs cells in two places

The package list includes the factory assembled chassis, completed body, wheels and tires, KT-531P radio, tread adjustment pieces, pinion gears, wheel nuts and the two small tools. That is enough to begin without painting or buying radio electronics. Kyosho’s official photograph showing a transmitter alongside the car is useful because the body-only versions can look nearly identical in a close-up.

The simple startup plan is four alkaline AAA cells for the chassis and four for the handset. The product page has inconsistent wording about rechargeable cells. One equipment block puts the NiMH option with the transmitter, while another puts it with the chassis. Its introduction describes alkaline cells for both, and the operating manual linked by the exact listing supplies the reference for the radio and chassis. The budget sticks to the common alkaline basis. Check Kyosho’s instructions for the specific compartment before choosing rechargeables.

Keep the small tools and gears in a labeled bag. A used Readyset may have a different pinion or wheel set fitted, and those changes can matter more than a missing cosmetic insert when you want to get back to the factory arrangement. Confirm the included radio and stock parts separately when you compare the price of a used example.

03 /

A brushed rear-drive car with a geared differential

The installed 130-class brushed motor turns the rear axle through the pinion, spur and gear differential. The differential lets the two driven wheels rotate at different speeds through a turn. The front tires guide the car and carry no drive in this configuration. Those facts matter if you are comparing it with an AWD MINI-Z sold for drifting or with a brushless MR-04 EVO2. Each serves a different setup and purchase plan.

Kyosho includes 6T, 7T, 8T and 9T pinions. At the same driven gear, a larger pinion reduces the numerical reduction. The effect on a real session also depends on the available space, batteries, traction and motor load. Keep the installed gear as a reference while you learn the circuit. If you choose a different pinion, use the matching motor-guide arrangement and check the manual for the actual rear case.

Full bearings and a geared differential are present as supplied. That gives you a documented standard to work from when the car starts to roll less freely. Fine fibers or dirt around an axle can still create drag. With power disconnected, inspect the wheels and rear assembly before attributing uneven acceleration to the motor. A well-labeled baseline pays off when you have multiple MINI-Z chassis and a drawer of gears.

04 /

Use the KT-531P to make the first laps repeatable

Kyosho says the car starts in training mode. Begin by finding neutral and checking steering direction according to the manual. Throttle trim, steering trim and steering dual rate perform different jobs. Trim centers the response, while steering rate changes the available command range. Record the settings you actually use so that you can return to them if the car begins to push wide or feels too sensitive.

The supplied KT-531P is part of this Readyset’s radio system. An older transmitter with MINI-Z branding does not establish compatibility. Compare the protocols and the receiver instructions before counting an existing handset as a replacement. For a buyer with no radio equipment, the advantage is a known starting pair that arrives with the car.

Kyosho identifies MZW711 as an optional gyro. Its assistance is separate from steering trim and is absent from the standard equipment list. I would use the supplied controls first. A car that drifts off line at gentle pace needs a check for rubbing, uneven tire seating or a steering trim error before adding assistance. A local stock class may also have a rule about installed electronics and gyro use.

05 /

The wide LM layout determines more than wheelbase

The exact product page gives 102.0 mm wheelbase, 67.5 mm front tread and 65.0 mm rear tread. The offset references are narrow +3.0 mm in front and wide +1.0 mm at the rear. Front and rear width labels describe the wheels as well as how they sit on the hubs. Ordering four wheels on the strength of the number stamped on one of them can leave a tire touching the body even if the axle spacing is right.

The MR-04 uses front knuckles with integrated kingpins and an upper arm arrangement that changes camber through travel. Kyosho describes lower cell placement than the MR-03, plus a coil spring damper at the rear and a T-bar that supports the rear pod. The manufacturer’s design description is a starting point for understanding the car. Actual behavior will also depend on the body clearance and the surface you use.

If you swap a body or wheels, check that both front tires clear the shell under steering and suspension travel. Check the rear too. The supplied front tread parts do not turn every mounting arrangement into a direct fit. Kyosho notes that the motor case and differential gear assembly appropriate to a different body may be required. Keep the original wheel and mount information with the car rather than starting the conversion from a photo.

06 /

Choose lane width and edge clearance before judging the gearing

The Jaguar is 177.0 mm long, 79.5 mm wide and 44.0 mm tall in the manufacturer data. The size is small enough to make an indoor course practical. The long nose and rear wing still need room at the barriers, especially if the layout was marked out around a short-bodied car. Start with a clear curve and a straight that ends in room to stop. Notice where the tail travels before tightening a corner.

Use clean, flat indoor flooring or a suitable miniature RC circuit. Dust, fibers and threshold strips can matter at this size. On a shared home floor, choose a route away from people and furniture edges. On a club layout, ask about approved tires and whether the exact LM body and wheelbase fit the class. A body that is eligible at one track may belong in another group elsewhere.

For practice, keep the same surface and run an easy line until the car returns through each corner consistently. That gives you a fair basis for judging changes to steering rate or grip. A longer car that touches an inside barrier may simply need a wider route. Turning the steering control up does little if the front tire is already rubbing on the shell.

Two simple markers at the outside of a broad turn can help you judge room for the tail without making the markers part of the driving surface. Leave the course wide enough that you can make several slow laps without clipping an edge. Once that is repeatable, narrow one corner at a time. That is a better way to discover whether your available floor suits the Jaguar’s long shell than choosing gearing before you know how much of the room is usable.

07 /

Preserve the painted Jaguar and record its fit

The pink No.4 livery arrives painted and marked. It is a big part of the purchase, so keep the shell and wing out of contact with hard objects in the carry box. Kyosho also specifies a body-level limit: the separately sold MZW429R light kit cannot be installed on this Jaguar. Similar mounting descriptions elsewhere in the MINI-Z catalog do not override the note attached to 32369LE.

The manufacturer parts page helps identify the hardware compatible with this exact Readyset. It is more useful than searching on the common MR-04 name alone, particularly for a rear assembly that needs to fit a 102 mm chassis. When removing the body, follow the body mounting instructions and avoid treating the wing as a handle. After a barrier hit, inspect the body seating, wheel clearance and retention before blaming the alignment.

Store the handset and chassis with their cells removed according to the manual’s storage instructions. Keep the pinion and wheel tools in the same case. If you own a short-wheelbase Porsche or another MR-04, label the Jaguar’s stock wheel offsets in your setup notes. That small habit makes it much easier to restore the complete 32369LE configuration later.

Kyosho’s option list also names Jaguar-specific front bumper and rear wing choices. That is a clue to how much the long body drives the fit decisions. If the stock shell starts to sit crooked after contact with a barrier, check its front mount and rear retention before assuming the whole chassis is bent. Take a photo of the way the body sits on the wheels when everything is straight. It will be a useful comparison if the nose later moves closer to one tire.

The resulting USD 201.99 is a dated startup example before tax, shipping or any facility cost. The allowance is a planning amount. That amount assumes you have a place to drive. If the draw is regular club laps, ask about track access and class eligibility before adding optional gear to the cart. If you want a home circuit, the size and cost of that layout depend on the floor you have. An entry price that includes cells alone cannot answer either venue question.

Frequent use may lead you to investigate a supported rechargeable setup. Confirm the exact cell permissions for the chassis and handset, then account for both the cells and a suitable charger. Keep that as a separate budget from the alkaline start so the different requirements stay visible.

10 /

Buy 32369LE if you want the long prototype configuration

I would shortlist the Jaguar for an indoor driver with an appropriate layout and a reason to want the pink No.4. The included radio, assembled chassis and finished body make the initial purchase manageable. The 102 mm wheelbase, wheel offsets and lighting restriction are the details I would check against the plan before buying, because those are the ones that distinguish this release from the shorter options.

For a racer, class fit matters more than the appeal of the color. For a collector who intends to drive, check that the shell has enough space through the entire course. If the answer is yes, the exact Readyset brings a coherent package with an accessible alkaline start and enough documented geometry to keep it in its intended configuration.
