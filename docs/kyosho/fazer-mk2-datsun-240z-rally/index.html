The Datsun 240Z Rally Readyset is for a vintage-rally fan who wants the car assembled, the body finished and a more deliberate mild-dirt package than an ordinary touring Fazer. The reviewed car is 34482T1 on the FZ02-R platform, verified by Amazon under that same part number. It is not the standard white 240Z touring car, a replacement rally body or a general Fazer chassis. Drivetrain and power: Fazer Mk2 1971 Datsun 240Z Rally

The FZ02-R is shaft-driven full-time 4WD with gear differentials front and rear. The supplied G14L is brushed, and the manufacturer's stock ratio is 6.54:1. This is a conventional powered-four-wheel package for road and mild-dirt driving, not the rear-wheel-drive configuration of a modern dedicated drift car. The red rally body tells you the subject; the actual shaft and differentials tell you the mechanism.

Sealed gearbox housings and full bearings are useful documented features. They reduce direct contamination paths and provide a complete running assembly, but do not make the entire car maintenance-free. Inspect exposed drive joints and free wheel rotation after dusty running. A caught stone or binding suspension shaft should be dealt with mechanically rather than overcome by adding power.

The standard motor and gearing should remain the first reference point. A brushless conversion can change the operating demands and the value calculation, and we have no test supporting one here. If faster power is already a non-negotiable objective, compare a complete car sold with that system before purchasing an RTR only to replace its electronics. I would choose this Datsun when its stock rally equipment and finished body are the desired package. Its reason for being is a coordinated mild-rally car, rather than a cheap entrance to an undefined motor, battery and suspension conversion.

Radio and setup: Fazer Mk2 1971 Datsun 240Z Rally

The KT-231P+ transmitter is supplied with the installed radio system. Follow its documented neutral and trim setup, confirm steering direction and check the speed controller's startup and brake behavior before driving. A short first-session preparation is the benefit of a Readyset, but the presence of a finished shell does not establish that a control check can be omitted.

Set steering travel against the actual hardware range with the body installed. Watch for tire rubbing and a servo that keeps pushing after the wheels reach a stop. Check the pack lead and cover arrangement after every battery change so that wiring stays clear of the center drive and front linkage. If tracking changes, inspect the tires, linkage and debris before relying on more electronic trim.

Steering and suspension: Fazer Mk2 1971 Datsun 240Z Rally

Kyosho documents rally-specific shock cases, pistons and towers to provide the FZ02-R suspension arrangement. Oil dampers are fitted, and the altered towers support the model’s mild-rally brief. These are substantive equipment differences from a generic touring package. They do not establish measured bump control or guarantee that every pothole will be absorbed without damage.

The stock wheelbase is 260 mm and the tires are listed at 70 mm diameter and 28 mm width. That tire size is a meaningful difference from the smaller VTC road tires on some other Fazer bodies. Keep the original wheel offset and body clearance in view when ordering replacements. Another tire that fits a wheel may still rub the shell or alter the intended operating load.

Check suspension motion on both sides and preserve the stock setup before altering ride height or damping. If you adjust one item, use the same route to observe the effect. Changing gearing, tires and shocks together makes the result hard to interpret. The practical buyer benefit is a supplied rally configuration that can be understood and serviced, rather than the need to assemble one from touring-car options. Our score credits that documented starting equipment, not a measured steering or suspension win over another complete rally car.

Where to drive: Fazer Mk2 1971 Datsun 240Z Rally

Plan on pavement and comparatively even dry dirt. The cover, tires and rally shock hardware create a credible mild-rally brief, but scale still matters. Deep gravel, tall grass, large roots and broken ground can exceed what this low car's wheels and clearance can sensibly tackle. A dramatic rally photograph is not a universal ground recommendation, and the historical Datsun's success does not supply the model with full-size suspension travel.

Begin on a smooth area to verify tracking and braking, then move to a manageable dirt route with room to recover a turn. Use gentle undulations and open corners rather than jumps as the first experiment. Watch where the body and chassis contact the ground. If the car repeatedly catches, improve the route before deciding it needs more motor power or a large ride-height change.

After dusty use, remove the appropriate covers and inspect the joints, shocks, wheels and electronics area. The protective cover needs correct placement to be useful, and trapped debris should not be ignored because the model is marketed for rallying. Follow the actual instructions for water use and cleaning. We have no measured runtime, dust-ingress result or long-term reliability test here. A dry repeatable practice route gives the owner clearer feedback about stock setup and lets the finished red body remain part of the enjoyment instead of becoming a constant repair job.

Body and service: Fazer Mk2 1971 Datsun 240Z Rally

The red 1971 rally shell, auxiliary-lamp appearance and period details are the Datsun's strongest visual reasons to buy. It is finished and mounted on the complete car, eliminating clear-body trimming and paint work. That is real value for someone whose preferred first project is preparing a running model and creating a mild-rally route rather than reproducing the livery from scratch.

The manufacturer lists 473 mm length, 194 mm body width and 142 mm height. Those dimensions help with transport and shelf planning, but exposed details need room beyond a tight box. Remove the body using its intended mounts and avoid lifting the car by lamps or mirrors. Optional LED units can add illumination later; the ability to accept them is not proof that they are factory-fitted in the normal Readyset.

The gallery shows official whole-car views with optional illumination, clearly labeled as that manufacturer's example configuration. They are not our night-driving photographs or evidence that we tested the LEDs. A body-off or technical layout can also explain the FZ02-R platform without borrowing an unrelated touring test. I would preserve the stock shell during the first ownership stage and keep routine battery access simple. The detailed rally identity is more satisfying when it remains convenient to remove for the cleaning and inspection that mild-dirt running will require.
