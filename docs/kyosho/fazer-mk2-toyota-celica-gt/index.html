Drivetrain and power: Fazer Mk2 1970 Toyota Celica GT Orion Turquoise

The FZ02 is shaft-driven full-time 4WD with front and rear gear differentials. It uses a G14L brushed motor and a listed 6.54:1 stock ratio. Full ball bearings are documented throughout the running gear. This is a coherent conventional road-car package and a different mechanism from the rear-drive Fazer D2, even though both use realistic Toyota bodies and the broader Fazer name.

Gear differentials let the wheels on an axle turn at different speeds through a bend. The center shaft connects the two ends mechanically. Those facts explain the drivetrain without making a claim about measured grip or cornering balance. A buyer who wants drifting should not assume that ordinary 4WD and vintage tires produce the same discipline as a purpose-built RWD drift chassis with a gyro. Choose the category first and use the body to select within it.

The fixed-position motor mount supports a documented range of pinions, which makes later gearing work understandable. Keep the installed ratio for initial driving. Higher gearing is not a free performance improvement and should be considered only with the motor, battery, ground and operating instructions in view. I would inspect free wheel rotation and transmission seating before treating the car as underpowered. For this buyer, preserving a predictable standard road setup is more useful than beginning ownership with an electronics and gearing conversion that was never needed for the original scale-driving brief.

Steering and suspension: Fazer Mk2 1970 Toyota Celica GT Orion Turquoise

Independent suspension and oil-filled shocks are supplied, together with a fitted KS202W servo. That is useful equipment for a road-car owner who wants to understand adjustments without first purchasing suspension electronics and damping hardware. It is not proof that every stock setting will suit every pavement surface. Begin by checking that both sides move freely, that the shocks are secure and that the wheels clear the body through steering travel.

The stock wheelbase is 260 mm and the manufacturer lists an approximate 7 mm chassis height. Those numbers describe a low road platform. Raising the suspension arbitrarily does not by itself produce a documented rally configuration. If rough-dirt driving is already the plan, compare an FZ02-R rally Readyset instead of treating ride height as a minor change that settles every tire, body and debris-protection requirement.

For later adjustment, note the original settings and keep a consistent practice route. A new set of wheels can alter track and body clearance, so check offset and tire width rather than choosing by appearance alone. The brake-disc-style hubs and calipers are scale details, not functioning disc brakes. They add visual interest without changing the fundamental setup checks. Our rating credits the documented equipment and service understanding; it does not assign a measured steering-response or suspension-performance number to the stock Celica.

Where to drive: Fazer Mk2 1970 Toyota Celica GT Orion Turquoise

Plan on clean pavement or another smooth on-road area. Vintage Touring Car tires and the low body create a scale street-car brief. Cracked tarmac, grit and raised expansion joints are worth checking before the first run. A surface that is merely walkable can still be awkward for a low RC car, particularly if the route leaves no room to avoid a sharp edge.

Use the first battery to learn smooth starts, braking and broad turns. Avoid assuming that a relatively modest brushed package cannot reach obstacles quickly. Drive at a pace where the car’s position remains easy to judge, then inspect it before extending the session. We have not measured its speed or stopping distance, so the advice is about building a useful personal baseline instead of supplying a performance claim.

Dusty pavement is described in the manufacturer’s sealed-transmission discussion, but sealed gears do not make the entire chassis immune to dirt. Check shock shafts, drive joints, wheel rotation and the electronics area after running. Keep tire surfaces clean enough to make repeated driving observations meaningful. For wet use, follow the actual controller, radio and maintenance instructions, not just a waterproof-servo label. I would use dry predictable ground for initial ownership because it suits the scale-car role and reduces the number of changing conditions you must interpret while learning the stock setup.

Buying verdict: Fazer Mk2 1970 Toyota Celica GT Orion Turquoise
