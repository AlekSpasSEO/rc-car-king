01 /

Grave Digger is the attraction, and solid axles are the reason to stay.

The Mini LMT Grave Digger is a compact Monster Jam-style truck built around four-link solid axles and licensed BKT tires. Those features give LOS01026T1 a mechanical reason to exist beyond its green body. I would shortlist it for a small freestyle course and the Grave Digger presentation, provided the available ground suits a 277mm-long truck. The larger brushless LMT is a separate purchase: its running videos and power system cannot establish what this brushed mini will do.

02 /

The box includes the small truck and its power system.

Losi lists an 810mAh Smart G2 2S pack, USB-C charger, SLT2 radio and AA transmitter cells with the assembled truck. That is a substantial part of its appeal to a Monster Jam fan starting in hobby RC. You can concentrate on learning the vehicle instead of selecting a motor, receiver and battery independently. Confirm the charging input and original connector when unpacking, and use the supplied 2S arrangement as the reference before buying an extra battery.

This is a coordinated 2S brushed package, including its transmitter and finished body, rather than a roller needing several electronic purchases. Check what can be reused only after examining the supplied charging input and plug; the Smart G2 name is not a reason to buy an unrelated charger or pack.

03 /

The 380 brushed motor belongs to this exact mini.

The 25T 380-size brushed motor drives all four wheels through a 25A combined receiver/controller. Front and rear bevel differentials allow wheel-speed differences, so these are not locked crawler axles. Metal differential parts, yokes and links make the hardware identifiable for servicing; they do not establish how far the truck can be jumped without damage. The two-position wheelie bar offers a specific setup choice to explore with stock power before turning the purchase into a brushless conversion.

04 /

Reduced throttle is a practical freestyle training tool.

The SLT2 limiter can make the first sessions calmer while a new driver learns trigger control. That is particularly useful for a tall truck that invites sudden acceleration and theatrical maneuvers. Greg Vogel’s RC Driver video includes a drivetrain teardown and outside running on dirt and at a skatepark. It demonstrates his review work, not a set of RC Car King crash or handling results. I would use the teardown to understand the hardware while keeping our recommendation grounded in package fit.

The limiter also gives a shared family truck a practical starting setting. Before changing it, let the new driver learn to stop at a chosen mark and approach a low ramp without a last-second throttle stab. That is a course-specific use for the supplied radio, not a claim of electronic stability assistance.

05 /

The links and sway bars explain why this is a monster truck.

Four-link solid axles and sway bars give the Mini LMT a different suspension layout from an independently suspended Mini-T. Wheel movement involves the axle and chassis together, which is part of the monster-truck appeal. Threaded oil-filled shocks give the owner ride-height and damping adjustments to learn. I would first check link movement and steering travel with the truck assembled. Adding heavier metal parts before diagnosing a bind spends money without addressing why the linkage cannot move freely.

06 /

Use small ramps with room to finish the maneuver.

Losi’s recorded surface suggestions include garage floors, low-pile carpet, smooth dirt and suitable paved outdoor spaces. The 206mm width and BKT chevron tires belong in the plan for a compact freestyle course, rather than a promise of easy deep-lawn use. Start with a low ramp that has a shallow entrance and an unobstructed landing. Learning repeatable approaches on that course is a more useful first objective than attempting the flips shown in promotional footage.

07 /

The flip-top cage is an ownership feature, not just a decoration.

The Mini LMT uses a twist-lock flip-top cage, a molded roll structure and a finished Grave Digger body. Easy access is useful because battery changes and drivetrain inspections are routine. The official photo shows the compact layout that an owner will work around. Keep the body details, light leads and cage fixings secure after a tumble. We do not call the shell indestructible or infer long-term crack resistance from its licensed appearance; replacement diagrams and careful inspection are the practical resources.

09 /

The Rival MT10 solves a different outdoor problem.

Team Associated’s red Rival MT10 V2 20518 is a 1/10 four-wheel-drive brushless monster truck with independent suspension. It is considerably wider and longer than the Mini LMT and requires its own drive battery and charger. Consider it when the available ground and desired outdoor pace justify a larger vehicle. Choose the Mini LMT when compact solid-axle freestyle and Monster Jam detail are the intended experience. Neither format is a measured winner here; these are distinct packages for distinct spaces.

The Rival is therefore a change in both vehicle size and startup basket. Its separate drive-power requirement matters as much as its brushless label. If the goal is specifically a small solid-axle Grave Digger, a larger truck with a faster motor does not replace that format.

10 /

It is a good miniature monster purchase with realistic terrain expectations.

For a Monster Jam fan with a small freestyle course, the Mini LMT brings the relevant package together: Grave Digger body, solid axles, BKT tires and included 2S drive equipment. Its chief limitation is terrain scale. Choose it for compact monster-truck maneuvers on appropriate ground, with the stock power system and conservative ramps as the starting point. A larger independent-suspension truck deserves the shortlist when the regular driving area is much rougher. Our editorial rating evaluates those purchase choices, without claiming a crash or long-term reliability test.
