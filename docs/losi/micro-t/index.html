01 /

A short truck makes a different kind of indoor course.

The silver Micro-T is appealing when the truck body is the point of the purchase and the available circuit is compact. Its 102mm wheelbase and 145mm overall length differ from the longer Micro-B, even though both use a small brushed rear-drive format. I would choose LOS-1392T2 for its stadium-truck proportions and build a course around that size. The useful advantage is finding a place to drive at home; the truck shape does not make household steps and furniture-sized drops suitable obstacles.

02 /

The current Micro-T includes its own power equipment.

The LOS-1392T2 package supplies a 350mAh 2S LiPo and USB-C charger along with the assembled truck and SLT2 transmitter. That saves a beginner from choosing separate electronics before discovering whether micro racing is enjoyable. Keep the original pack as the reference when selecting a spare. The old Losi Micro-T name dates back to a different product generation, so an old charger, replacement motor or review should not be assumed to describe the current truck.

The recorded completion list calls the USB-C power supply optional. Keep that distinction when comparing the Micro-T with minis whose supplied USB charger explicitly requires an additional adapter. Buying a complete truck should simplify the first session, rather than trigger an unrelated battery and electronics shopping list.

03 /

Two-wheel drive rewards a gentler trigger finger.

The Losi 65T brushed motor drives the rear wheels, with speed control and radio reception handled by a combined 4A unit. That leaves the factory electronics matched to the supplied 2S pack. On a small circuit, the first useful adjustment is how gently the driver applies power out of a corner. A motor swap would spend money without solving a course that is too short, while changing the radio system needs planning around the integrated receiver/controller.

04 /

The speed switch lets the truck fit the room.

The SLT2 throttle limiter is an unusually practical part of this purchase. Begin at the reduced output setting and widen the course before using full output. The goal should be to finish several clean laps with consistent braking points. The outside 1I-RC article includes running impressions and agrees with the advertised speed estimate, but that is not a timed RC Car King speed measurement. There is no need to turn an indoor beginner recommendation into a speed-ranking exercise.

At only 145mm long, the truck can have room to circulate in places where a larger model cannot. The limiter is useful for making those laps deliberate: use a setting that leaves time to brake before the next corner, then open the circuit before increasing output.

05 /

Short wheelbase changes the setup question.

The Micro-T wheelbase is 102mm, against the Micro-B’s 118mm. That 16mm difference helps distinguish two vehicles that can look similarly tiny in photographs; it does not establish which turns faster or lands better. Oil-filled shocks give the owner a damping system to maintain, supported by an aluminum chassis. Before buying tuning parts, I would check the small steering connections and shock movement after barrier contact. These are more specific inspection jobs than treating the whole truck as a disposable toy.

Sources: Manufacturer ↗ · Manufacturer manual ↗ · Independent coverage ↗

06 /

Make the jump smaller than your enthusiasm.

The useful terrain is low-pile indoor surfaces or smooth outdoor space, with ramps designed for the Micro-T’s small wheels. Its Pro-Line truck tires and 109mm overall width do not turn it into a lawn vehicle. A jump with an abrupt entrance can act like a curb, so I would use a shallow transition and broad landing zone. If friends already run a micro track, check their surface and wheel rules before ordering replacement tires; the stadium-truck appearance alone cannot establish a matching setup.

07 /

The silver body is a finished starting point.

The supplied shell gives the truck its stadium appearance without a painting job. For someone who wants a small vehicle to drive between other projects, that is a practical part of the package. Remove and replace the shell carefully around the compact wiring rather than forcing it down when something catches. The model-specific drawing and manual are better repair references than a generic Micro-T search, which can include legacy parts and unrelated bodies.

09 /

The Micro-B is the more useful comparison than an upgrade motor.

Losi Micro-B LOS00007T1 is a complete current buggy with a 118mm wheelbase, an included 2S package and an aluminum chassis. Its longer buggy proportions and tire presentation create a different buying route. Pick the Micro-T for the truck body and shorter footprint; pick the Micro-B when you want a micro buggy circuit or friends already use that format. We have not raced them against each other and do not infer a lap-time advantage from a scale label.

Because both options include drive power, the decision is chiefly about the truck or buggy layout and the course you can use. The Micro-B’s longer wheelbase is a concrete distinction; spending first on a faster Micro-T motor does not reproduce the buggy’s proportions or put it in the same local class.

10 /

The recommendation depends on a usable small circuit.

For someone who wants indoor stadium-truck driving without selecting separate electronics, the Micro-T has a clear buying case. The short footprint, supplied 2S equipment and reduced-throttle option support that use. Its main limitation is asking a 145mm truck to do a larger outdoor vehicle’s job. The independent article discusses a 20-plus-minute battery claim, while the recorded official wording says 25-plus; neither establishes our measured runtime. Choose it for a compact circuit and the truck format, with that evidence limit understood.
