01 /

A rat rod with short-course hardware underneath

Start with what you want to see crossing the pavement. The exposed wheels, chopped black shell and hot-rod proportions are the reasons to choose this Pro2 over a conventional short-course truck. Underneath is an assembled 1/10 rear-drive platform with adjustable links and a separate radio system. That makes this a model for someone who wants to drive a distinctive street car and still have enough adjustment to restore the setup after a hard meeting with a barrier.

The name matters at checkout. The black 70024 is the base truck, and 70024C adds the drive battery and charger. A storefront can show the same body photo for either. Associated explicitly separates the two in its product comparison, and Horizon identifies the combo under distributor code ASC70024C. Treat those characters as part of the shopping list, especially if the price seems unusually close to the base truck or the listing photo shows only the body.

There is a worthwhile distinction between street driving and speed-run shopping. The rat rod brings a brushless rear-drive system, but its value is a small collection of ordinary turns, launches and stops that you can repeat in the same open space. The available setup adjustments let you work on those inputs. If you mainly want a large open field, the published street layout and exposed corners make a different complete truck a more sensible match.

02 /

The C suffix changes what you take to the bench

Horizon’s exact combo description lists the assembled rat rod, a 3300 mAh 30C 7.4 V Reedy pack and the Reedy 324-S compact balance charger. Associated identifies the battery connector as a T-plug and calls for four AA cells in the handset. There is no body painting or motor installation on the path to a first drive. The owner still needs a suitable charging location and the supplied charger instructions for the specific pack.

The 324-S is an AC charger. Before ordering an archived or used combo, ask for a photo of the actual pack, charger and packaging label. A photo of the truck beside a charger proves little if a seller has mixed a base 70024 listing with the combo images. The separately listed charger product page documents the Reedy combination, while the truck manual describes what the installed speed control accepts. Use both when a secondhand package includes substituted equipment.

With the radio cells in place, the battery straps and foam placement deserve a look before the body goes back on. The manual shows that the foam can move fore or aft in the tray. Check that the pack stays secure without a lead pressing on the body, and record the position before experimenting. That provides a known configuration for evaluating the effect of any later suspension adjustment.

03 /

The stock gearing has a reference you can restore

The Reedy 3300Kv motor sends power to the rear through an externally adjustable slipper and a sealed gear differential. The 2.6:1 internal ratio combines with the documented 18-tooth pinion and 87-tooth spur for the manual’s 12.57:1 final drive figure. That is a practical identification check after buying a used car or replacing a damaged gear. A faster-looking pinion in a used truck can explain a setup that no longer matches the quick-start sheet.

The slipper and differential solve different problems. The slipper can give briefly under a sudden load at the transmission input. The differential lets the inside and outside rear wheels turn at different speeds in a corner. A tire breaking loose on a dusty patch, a long slipper flare and a rough gear mesh can sound or feel similar from the driver’s position. Inspect each mechanism before tightening the first adjustment you can reach.

Keep the combo’s supplied 2S pack as the review baseline. The RT10SW instructions mention a smaller 17-tooth pinion, part 1335, as the starting point for 3S and also tie the choice to surface and venue. The manual’s stock gearing table describes the 3300Kv installation. It does not convert the optional 3S route into a drop-in shopping recommendation. If you change power later, check the exact controller, gearing, fit and charger documentation together.

04 /

Give the radio and steering assist separate jobs

The RTR comes with a two-channel radio and a DVC receiver. The radio sets what you ask for at the wheel and trigger, while DVC can add a steering correction based on the car’s movement. A car that needs mechanical centering can still look tidier with assistance active. Establish the stock steering center and check both directions first, then use the XP120 DVC manual for any change to the assist.

Start with the wheels and trim as the manufacturer instructs and use a broad path with room to stop. Change one setting between comparisons. If you add steering input and gain together, a tidier first corner tells you little about which change helped. A dry piece of pavement with a clear neutral return makes an easier place to recognize a steering correction than an uneven surface with ruts pushing the front wheels around.

The manual identifies the steering servo saver and the linkage hardware. If the car starts pulling after contact, check the saver and the front links before adding more correction. A ball cup or steering link can shift even when the receiver still responds normally. With drive power disconnected, compare left and right play. Restore the mechanical baseline before treating assistance as a cure for tracking.

05 /

Use the links to keep a repeatable baseline

Steel turnbuckles provide camber and front toe adjustment, and vertical ball ends give the owner more geometry choices. Those are meaningful options on an RTR with this much pavement emphasis. Take a photo of the mounting points and write down the settled ride height before touching a link. A camber measurement from a truck hanging off a stand is difficult to compare with the same car resting on its tires.

The 12 mm aluminum coil-over shocks and adjustable body mounts are two different ways to change where things sit. Use the shock setup to restore the chassis attitude documented for your own car. Inspect the body mounts separately if only the shell rubs. Altering the suspension to move a misaligned shell can sacrifice useful travel while leaving the real mounting issue in place.

Short-course ancestry is helpful when reading the illustrations, but it can also tempt a buyer to assume interchangeability. Use the RT10SW drawings when ordering a damaged link, mount or bumper. Hobbydex’s platform record flags shared hardware across Pro2 variants and points to the specific RT10SW documentation. That is a useful clue for searching diagrams, with the manufacturer part number as the deciding check.

If the steering looks straight at neutral and one wheel still rubs at full lock, compare the body posts and the steering endpoints before shortening a link. The RT10SW’s exposed wheels make the left and right clearance easy to inspect. Remeasure toe after a link change. That extra bench check is especially useful when the car has had a new steering cup fitted on only one side.

06 /

Give the rear tires a consistent surface

Associated lists this car as on-road and supplies street-stock style wheels and all-terrain tires. I would choose clean pavement as the regular venue and use firm, level hardpack only when the chassis and body have room over the surface. A dusty seam across the course matters because it changes available grip while the rear wheels are feeding in power. Repeating the same corner on a cleaner line makes it easier to tell surface changes from setup changes.

Leave more stopping room than the 492.4 mm body length suggests. Start a turn before the available pavement runs out, leave the throttle gentle as the front wheels take a set, and check the tire edges after crossing loose material. A wider initial course also gives the exposed corners some margin if a rear tire lets go on the exit. That is an appropriate use of the included pack without relying on a published top-speed claim.

This truck is also more interesting on a familiar surface than on a curb-to-curb run where you have to lift immediately. Put down a pair of soft markers and decide where you will slow down. A few consistent passes offer a better starting point for steering settings and rear tire wear than jumping to a new motor or higher voltage after one slide.

07 /

Protect the mounting points and label the parts that fit

The factory shell is a large part of this purchase. The wheels sit exposed at the edges and the body height is adjustable. After taking the shell off, check the post locations and fasteners while you can see them. Before the next drive, turn both ways and check tire clearance through ordinary suspension movement. Restoring the old body height after service is easier when you have a reference photo.

The rear CVA drive shafts, gear cover and slipper should go on a simple inspection list. With power disconnected, check for trapped material around the axle and for uneven movement from side to side. The manual gives an exploded path to the parts. A rough corner after a dusty run is a reason to inspect the hardware before buying a complete differential or changing settings to cover the symptom.

If you are deciding on an older boxed combo, find the right body and running hardware in the parts diagram before paying. Horizon marks its combo listing discontinued, while the maker’s base-product page still describes the C package. That difference is a reason to verify the actual carton and replacement part source locally. It does not establish what an independent seller has on the shelf.

09 /

Choose by the surface and body you intend to drive

The Team Associated Pro2 SC10SW RTR 70041 is the closer mechanical comparison if your interest is short-course practice. Its body and wheel package are the point of that choice. Use its own startup sheet because the standard 70041 requires a battery and charger. For a class, ask about body and equipment rules before assuming a street rat rod and short-course truck can share the same entry.

The Team Associated Reflex 14R Hoonicorn gives you a smaller complete pavement car with a different drivetrain. Compare the two packages if storage and a compact paved layout drive your purchase. The rat rod makes more sense when you have room for its larger body, want rear-drive throttle practice and like the visible wheels.

The Kyosho Fazer Mk2 El Camino is a scale-street alternative with shaft-driven 4WD. It makes sense if you want the classic road-car shape and prefer a four-wheel-drive layout. Keep its pack requirements and wheelbase separate when comparing the final bill. The Team Associated’s adjustable rear-drive platform and included Reedy equipment are the reasons to come back to 70024C.

10 /

Buy the 70024C when the included equipment solves a real need

I would choose this combo for a driver with suitable pavement who likes the rat-rod shape and needs a complete 2S power setup. Verify the exact carton and look for the battery and charger in the written contents. You can then spend the first few runs learning the rear-drive layout and keeping a record of the stock settings.

If you already own the right power equipment, give the 70024 a fresh price comparison. If the next thing you plan to do is enter a class, start with that class’s written requirements. The 70024C is easiest to justify when the shape, driving surface and included hardware all make sense together.
