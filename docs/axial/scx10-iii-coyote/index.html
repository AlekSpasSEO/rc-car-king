Axial SCX10 III Coyote review

A full-size SCX10 III purchase with a finished Coyote body and fitted radio. Drive battery and charger are extra; we assess the complete trail-ready shopping basket.

THE OVERVIEW

The blue SCX10 III Coyote is a 1/10 trail and crawling purchase with a deliberately narrower Pro-Line body and a competition-oriented equipment direction. It is more substantial than a micro crawler, so its attraction is room to explore real rock lines rather than desktop convenience. I would shortlist it for a driver who values the chassis and tire package more than a licensed full interior.

01 /

The simplified body suits a driver who wants to use the obstacles.

The blue AXI-2036T2 Coyote puts its budget into a narrower Pro-Line body and crawling hardware rather than a licensed full interior. I would shortlist it for outdoor rock lines where a 1/10 truck has room to approach and reverse. For indoor obstacles and easy storage, the SCX24 alternative below is a different purchase worth considering. This is the standard RTR; the battery-inclusive RTR Plus has a separate package identity.

02 /

The standard RTR stops before the battery and charger.

The assembled truck has fitted power electronics, radio and servo, but the drive battery and charger are extra for AXI-2036T2. Four AA transmitter cells are included, so they should not be counted as another required purchase. This precise distinction matters because Greg Vogel's RC Driver coverage discusses both the standard package and RTR Plus. Choose a compatible 2S or 3S LiPo with appropriate physical fit and charging equipment. A bundle photograph from the other package does not change this ASIN's inventory.

03 /

Portals and front overdrive are more useful than a power headline.

The Coyote uses AR45P portal axles, LCXU transmission and a 35T 540 brushed motor with Spektrum 40A control. Axial also documents front-axle overdrive. Portal gearing raises the axle center clearance, while overdrive changes the front-to-rear wheel-speed relationship. Those mechanisms can help a crawling setup in different ways, but we have not measured a climbing advantage. Keep the factory arrangement as the starting point and understand the required maintenance before treating each feature as an automatic competition result.

04 /

The receiver has more capacity than the basic transmitter.

The supplied SLT2 transmitter operates two driving channels. The SR515 receiver has additional channel capacity, but that does not add switches to the transmitter or make every optional function available. Before planning a conversion, identify the required control and compatible equipment. Greg Vogel's RC Driver coverage includes its own Coyote rock running; those observations belong to that publisher's sample.

Sources: Manufacturer ↗ · Manufacturer manual ↗ · Independent coverage ↗

05 /

The new mount positions are tools for an owner who takes notes.

Axial describes revised shock towers, panhard mounting, skid plate and boat-side trays alongside multi-link solid-axle suspension. Those changes provide concrete subjects to inspect rather than a vague claim that the Coyote is another Base Camp body. The oil shocks and stainless links still require checking for free motion through articulation. Preserve the factory positions before moving mounts, and investigate tire interference or steering bind at the source. More adjustments are useful only when you can explain which problem a change is intended to solve.

Sources: Manufacturer ↗ · Manufacturer manual ↗ · Independent coverage ↗

06 /

Trepador tires and dual-stage foams make a coherent rock package.

The Maxxis Trepador tires sit on KMC Grenade wheels with dual-stage open-cell inserts. The softer outer foam and firmer inner support are documented equipment choices, not measured grip figures. They help define the intended crawling purchase more clearly than the blue shell alone. Choose dry initial lines with room to approach and reverse, then inspect the tires and steering after the session. A portal axle does not eliminate every belly hang-up or prevent the vehicle from rolling on a steep side slope.

07 /

Clipless access should make the inspection routine easier.

The Pro-Line Coyote body removes without conventional clips and exposes the chassis for pack changes and maintenance. Its contoured shape is meant to provide obstacle and tire clearance, which is a practical reason to buy it even without a licensed full-scale badge. Check the latch fit and wiring after body removal, and avoid routing the battery lead where the shell can trap it. We have not measured body retention or impact resistance. The official manual and exploded view are the evidence for how the truck is assembled and serviced.

BEFORE YOU ORDER /

The questions worth asking.

Does AXI-2036T2 include a battery and charger?+

No. The standard blue RTR requires a compatible 2S or 3S LiPo and charger. Four AA transmitter cells are included. The battery-inclusive RTR Plus is a separate package.

Does the Coyote use portal axles?+

The documented package uses AR45P portal axles, an LCXU transmission and front-axle overdrive. These identify its crawling equipment; they do not establish a measured climbing advantage.

Can the included radio operate every SR515 receiver channel?+

The SLT2 provides two-channel driving controls. Extra receiver channels do not add controls to the supplied transmitter, so an optional function may require additional compatible equipment.

Why does the weight specification mention a battery?+

The manufacturer weight basis includes a 1300mAh pack, although the standard AXI-2036T2 excludes the drive battery. Do not read that weight note as an included-item list.

Would the SCX24 4Runner fit a smaller space better?+

Its 1/24 format and included power equipment make it a more compact route into crawling. Choose between that small-course package and the Coyote’s 1/10 footprint before comparing body details.

Has RC Car King tested its competition capability?+

No. The score assesses the documented purchase and service information. We have not measured climbing, grip or durability, and a buyer must check the intended class rules separately.
