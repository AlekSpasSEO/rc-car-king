THE OVERVIEW

The Bandit XL-5 24254-8 is for a first recreational buggy buyer who likes exposed wheels and has firm ground on which to learn a line. 02 /

A finished buggy means the first job is setup, not assembly.

Traxxas documents an assembled Bandit with its radio system and fitted electronics, a finished body and wing, 3000mAh seven-cell 8.4V NiMH battery, four-amp USB-C charger and maintenance tools. That gives a newcomer a coordinated starting point. You do not have to decide which motor and receiver to fit before exploring the hobby. The included power equipment also needs to stay visible in a price comparison: a cheaper-looking kit that needs electronics, finishing and charging hardware may be the larger first purchase.

The remaining required equipment includes four AA transmitter cells and a suitable USB-C power source. Traxxas recommends its 45-watt adapter and cable. Confirm the actual power-supply requirements from the manual rather than assuming every USB-C phone adapter gives the charger's intended output. The charger and its input supply are separate devices. That distinction is especially important for a gift, because opening a complete-looking box should not be followed by discovering that charging cannot begin with the equipment available at home.

Before connecting the pack, inspect battery retention, wheel nuts, suspension travel and wire routing. The slender body makes it easy to leave an incorrectly routed lead where it can interfere with a moving part. Follow the radio startup instructions and make a supported-wheel steering check. Save the first outdoor session for confirming neutral, steering direction and braking on simple ground. A carefully checked RTR is a shorter project than a kit, but it remains a real vehicle that benefits from a deliberate first setup.

03 /

A rear-driven buggy keeps the power path understandable.

The Titan 12T 550 feeds a Magnum 272 transmission with a slipper clutch and planetary rear differential. The front wheels are unpowered. This is a clear layout for learning where the motor, spur, gearbox and driveshafts sit in the vehicle. Rear drive also means the tires at the back provide both acceleration and a large part of the cornering work. That creates a useful reason to practice progressive throttle instead of expecting all steering corrections to come from a larger wheel angle.

Keep factory gearing while you establish the first baseline. The current official page lists 48-pitch gearing and a manufacturer speed claim above 35mph with the seven-cell battery. We have not measured that speed, and it is not a promise for grass, gravel or your particular route. Motor loading changes with grip, gearing and resistance from the ground. The internal cooling fan is documented equipment; it does not make prolonged running against an obstruction or through dragging vegetation a sensible test.

The transmission is described as brushless-ready, but the fitted vehicle is brushed. Budget any conversion as a complete project. A motor and controller are only part of the decision once battery chemistry, charging, mounting and associated drivetrain choices are included. If you already want a brushless buggy, compare complete factory alternatives before buying this model merely as a donor. The Bandit's stock-package value is strongest when you will use the coordinated equipment that comes with it.

04 /

Reduced power leaves more attention for direction and braking.

The TQ two-channel 2.4GHz radio and XL-5 controller supply a straightforward control arrangement. Sport, Race and Training profiles cover normal forward/brake/reverse use, running without reverse and reduced-power learning. Training reduces forward and reverse output while keeping braking. That is useful for a first buggy session because steering toward you reverses the visual intuition many newcomers bring from steering away. Less acceleration gives you more time to work out direction and neutral without needing another transmitter.

Make the initial circuit wide enough to recover from a missed turn. Practice stopping before linking fast corners or adding a jump. Changing the controller profile should be done using the exact LED procedure in the manual, preferably with the buggy stationary on the bench. This model does not have TSM stabilization. Your own throttle and steering choices remain the controlling inputs, which suits a buyer who wants to learn rear-drive behavior rather than delegate corrections to an assistance system.

If you later choose a compatible two-cell LiPo, revisit battery-type and low-voltage settings before driving. A pack that plugs in is not automatically ready to use correctly. The supplied NiMH arrangement is valuable because it lets you establish normal operation without changing several variables at once. It contains no measured radio range, response time or braking-distance result, and should not be confused with an Amazon customer score.

05 /

Watching the front wheels is part of this buggy's appeal.

The official specification identifies a 2056 servo, servo saver and independent suspension using oil-filled Ultra Shocks. Heavy-duty components are part of the current package. Check both front wheels through the steering travel and inspect that the linkage moves freely before making trim adjustments. Loose stones and grass near an exposed front assembly can create a mechanical problem that a radio adjustment cannot cure. Correct the obstruction or damaged component before asking the servo to work harder.

The front ribbed tires and rear step-pin pattern do different jobs. Their roles suit the rear-driven buggy layout, and they provide a useful starting point for learning how the surface changes the vehicle. Do not assume that a wider truck wheel will improve every aspect of the Bandit. Changing tire size or offset can affect clearance, steering load and gearing. Establish what the factory combination does on the intended ground before treating another model's wheel package as an automatic upgrade.

Use a consistent corner or low bump for setup comparisons. Record the original ride height and shock arrangement before making one change. If a change helps one part of the route and worsens another, you have learned something about the compromise rather than necessarily fitted the wrong component. We have no physical setup log for this exact blue buggy. The recommendation is to make adjustment a controlled learning process, supported by the current manual and drawings instead of an unverified internet recipe.

06 /

Keep the route proportionate to the wheels.

Firm dirt, smooth loose-surface practice and modest bumps are the first environments I would investigate. The Bandit has a narrower buggy footprint than the trucks in Traxxas's beginner range, and its wheels remain exposed. Look at the intended course at wheel height. Pebbles, ruts and roots that are minor to a person can be substantial to this car. A useful route has room for turns, recovery and stopping as well as a straight section that allows acceleration.

Thick lawn is the buying concern. If grass continuously drags against the buggy or catches near moving parts, the vehicle may spend more time struggling than teaching you a line. A monster truck is a real alternative when that is the only available terrain. Do not interpret a broad off-road label as permission to force any surface. The correct response to a mismatched yard can be another vehicle format, rather than more motor power and additional heat in the existing one.

Independent Bandit coverage from RC Cars Guide identifies the current XL-5 and distinguishes earlier VXL versions. It is a researched guide, not a hands-on durability study, so we use it for generation context. Older Bandit test figures are deliberately not carried into this review. The present recommendation is about matching the current documented package to a prepared recreational route, with the buyer's own observation providing the next stage of evidence after the first sessions.

07 /

The slender shell needs correct fit around a busy chassis.

The Bandit comes with a finished buggy body and wing, so the first purchase does not require paint and trimming equipment. That appearance is a real part of why a buggy enthusiast may choose it over a truck. Learn the body mounting arrangement shown in the current manual and check that the shell does not pinch wires or touch the drivetrain. Do not borrow a body-release procedure from a clipless truck simply because its radio and motor are related.

Exposed suspension helps you spot dirt and damage. After a run, look over the arms, wheel fasteners, tire bonding and rear shafts, then inspect the shock shafts and seals. Brush debris away before it has an opportunity to bind a corner during the next session. Check the motor and gear-cover area as well. Use the exact 24254-8 exploded views to identify replacement parts, because a familiar family resemblance does not guarantee that every old Bandit component is the current fitted item.

The electronic waterproofing claim should be read with the manual's operating and maintenance instructions. Wet use still requires care for mechanical assemblies and proper drying. We have no crash sequence or extended ownership record to justify calling the buggy unbreakable. Our service assessment rewards documentation and an understandable layout. Local stock remains something to check directly with the shop that would support you, especially when buying for a newcomer who may need help reading a parts drawing.

Another wheel format may solve the problem more directly.

The Traxxas Rustler XL-5 37254-8 is the clearest nearby alternative. It supplies a similarly coordinated brushed starter package with wider stadium-truck proportions. I would compare it when the intended ground is a little rougher or the owner wants truck styling and a broader tire footprint. The official dimensions support a format comparison; they do not establish which vehicle handles better. The Bandit remains attractive when the narrower buggy shape and visible open-wheel line are the activity you specifically want.

The Stampede XL-5 36254-8 moves farther toward monster-truck clearance. That is a worthwhile shift for a predominantly grassy or irregular yard, while rear-wheel drive remains part of the choice. Its tall stance changes the driving exercise as much as its appearance. Avoid buying the Bandit, fitting progressively taller truck wheels and then assuming the result is the same as choosing the appropriate factory format at the beginning.

For a larger four-wheel-drive brushless buggy, compare the ARRMA Typhon 223S complete RTR. Battery and charger are separate, and the 2S/3S power and DSC equipment create a different startup basket. It is neither a bare chassis nor an equivalent price-only substitution. Choose that route if the desired drivetrain and performance envelope are already clear. We have no head-to-head lap, speed or durability test to rank it over the Bandit.

10 /

A sensible first buggy for the buyer who wants this exact kind of driving.

I would shortlist the Bandit 24254-8 for a newcomer with a prepared firm-ground route who prefers buggy proportions and wants the supplied battery and charger. The fitted electronics, finished body, reduced-power option and service documents make the first project understandable. The reservation is more about use than glamour: dense grass and a predetermined competition class can each make another vehicle the smarter purchase. Recognize those conditions before turning a modest buggy into a large upgrade bill.

That is enough for a useful conditional buying judgment, but it remains distinct from a personal test or a quantified owner survey.

Start with the supplied battery, modest controls and a route you can repeat. Watch how the rear tires respond as you add throttle and keep notes before adjusting anything. If you need more clearance, first decide whether your route or the entire vehicle category should change. Buy the Bandit to enjoy an approachable rear-drive buggy as it comes. Skip it when your own requirements already describe a 4WD brushless truck, crawler or racing kit.

BEFORE YOU ORDER /

The questions worth asking.

Is the current reviewed Bandit a VXL?+

No. 24254-8 uses a Titan 12T brushed motor and XL-5. VXL power-system parts and older complete VXL vehicles are different purchases.

What remains to start driving?+

Four transmitter AA cells and suitable USB-C input equipment, plus the manual setup and charging procedure. The drive NiMH battery and charger are included.

Can truck tires automatically improve it?+

A size or offset change can alter clearance, steering load and effective gearing. Establish the stock setup on your ground before treating another model's wheels as a universal upgrade.

Is it appropriate for thick lawn?+

Our recommendation is a prepared firm route. Compare a monster truck when dragging grass is the only available surface.

Can it enter buggy racing?+

Ask the organizer about class and equipment rules. The Ready-To-Race branding does not guarantee eligibility at a particular venue.

What evidence supports the rating?+
