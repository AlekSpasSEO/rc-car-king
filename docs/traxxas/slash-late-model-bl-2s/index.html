01 /

Start with the local class sheet

The Fox Edition is for the person who sees a dirt oval and wants to put the same car through the same turn several ways. It is a 1/10 rear-drive car with a low chassis and a shell shaped for late-model racing. It shares the Slash name with a short-course truck, but it makes a different decision about what the tires, body and suspension should do. If you want to clear rough ground or set up jumps, that decision does not help you.

Check what your club runs before you buy. Ask whether the exact 104364-74 platform and BL-2s motor are eligible, whether it has a stock tire rule, and what it requires for the battery and timing transponder. A factory race profile or a licensed body design does not establish class legality. RC Driver’s launch feature shows the oval configuration and identifies the Fox livery among the variants. The rules still come from the track.

The black and orange shell is the Fox variant of the complete car, with factory electronics already installed. The box or listing should identify the complete 104364-74 model. A Fox body from the parts list can look almost identical in a storefront thumbnail. Verify the assembled vehicle and the exact variant together.

02 /

The power package and timing equipment are separate decisions

Traxxas supplies the assembled car with body, BL-2s motor and speed control, steering and TQ transmitter, plus the basic tools listed in the manual. The drive pack, charger and four transmitter AAs are separate. The manual supports a compatible 2S LiPo or the documented six- or seven-cell NiMH configuration. The startup example uses a compatible 2S setup. Match the chemistry, connector and charger to the exact pack, and use the controller’s documented battery protection configuration.

The extended tray accommodates different approved packs and lets you adjust battery position. That is useful because the pack has appreciable mass relative to the rest of the car. It still needs to be secured with the proper hold-down and any required spacer. Confirm that the lead exit and routing work without putting pressure on the body, linkages or gear cover.

The chassis provides a transponder mounting location, which is useful if you race. It is a place for timing hardware, not evidence that timing hardware is included or required at every club. Ask about a loaner or the club’s system before including a transponder in the purchase. If your class specifies a pack model or case style, use that in your quote instead of assuming the example below will pass tech.

03 /

Rear drive, gear differential and an adjustable slipper

The BL-2s 3300Kv sensorless motor sends drive to the rear through a Magnum 272R transmission. The gear differential allows different wheel speeds through a corner. The slipper clutch sits at the input and can limit the torque spike sent into that drivetrain. Those are separate mechanisms. If the rear tires light up on a dusty exit, use a gentler entry and exit at stock setup before deciding that the clutch or differential needs adjustment.

The manual’s internal reduction factor is 2.72 in its final-drive formula. The product page lists 9.10:1 under a label that calls it internal. Those figures should not be treated as two interchangeable gearbox reductions. If you are checking gearing against a class rule, identify the actual pinion and spur and use the manual’s stated method. Do not order a gear based only on the marketing table’s label.

With a stock motor and tires, set a useful baseline by running the same few laps before changing any gearing or clutch setting. A car that slows partway through a run can be encountering a protection mode or mechanical drag. Inspect it using the BL-2s status and maintenance instructions before drawing conclusions from the throttle response.

04 /

Use the race profile for reverse lockout where required

The BL-2s manual describes Sport, Race and Training profiles. The Race profile preserves forward and braking and disables reverse. The Training profile reduces forward and reverse command while retaining braking, and Sport is the documented default with reverse. Use the exact manual and check the club’s requirements before a heat. The name of the profile tells you what the controller will allow. It does not make the car corner differently by itself.

Check the battery protection indication separately from the driving profile. For the LiPo example, use the LiPo protection setting in the manual. If a car from a previous owner arrives with unknown settings, confirm the stock procedures and do the vehicle checks in a clear space before making any running assumption. A controller that gives no reverse in Race mode may be operating exactly as intended.

Keep the first session simple. Choose a repeatable place to lift before the turn, a point where you can let the car take a set and a point where you begin to feed in throttle. A marked line gives you something to compare when you return with another surface or setup.

05 /

Keep a written alignment and battery baseline

Adjustable links and multiple suspension settings can help a driver adapt the car to a local surface, but they also let two otherwise identical cars start from quite different places. Before making a change, record the axle positions, battery location and the link settings you can verify. If you inherit a used car, compare those against the factory manual and current class rules before trying to solve its behavior with extra steering trim.

The body gives a useful clue during a check. If the front tires brush the shell when turned and compressed, fix the fit or restore the correct wheel position before changing the toe. The parts sheet distinguishes front and rear wheel applications on the 2WD car. That is a useful reason to label the wheels when they come off, even when the tread looks similar across both axles.

A class check goes better if you can show what is still stock. Keep a photo of the mounted wheels, a note of the actual pack and a record of any alignment or body work. If the track permits a transponder, verify where its mount and lead sit with the shell closed. Use the existing mounting location and the timing system’s own instructions. Before your first heat, check with the club that the system counts your laps. This is easier to diagnose in practice than while other cars are on the clock.

The steering and battery positions affect different things. Steering geometry changes the direction and angle of the tire as it moves. Moving the battery changes where some of the mass sits. Change one variable within the rules, drive a consistent line and record whether you can reproduce the effect. The most useful setting is one you can restore.

06 /

The low shell earns its space on prepared dirt

A smooth oval has a repeatable entry and exit even when its grip changes over the course of an event. The low chassis and enclosed body suit that job. On broken ground or long grass, the low edges and covered wheels can run into material the stock Slash short-course body handles differently. Walk the route and find where the surface breaks, especially just off the line you intend to use.

The printed front and rear track measurements are 313 mm, and Traxxas lists a 656 mm length. That makes the car longer than the first glance at a 1/10 label suggests. Traxxas’s current product page pairs 333 mm with 12.3 inches for the wheelbase. Those values differ when converted, so confirm the physical dimension directly if you are fitting a storage tray, a body or a class gauge.

If the track offers practice time, use it to learn how grip changes across the corner. Begin with one steady line and see where the rear tires first begin to slide. An earlier, smoother lift can be more useful than making several chassis changes at once. A purpose-built car makes the most sense where you can repeat that experiment.

07 /

The Fox shell and oval wheels belong in the service notes

The orange and black body uses a clipless mounting system and covers the rear wheels. Check that its mounts and the side protection are properly seated, particularly after contact. Look for a wheel rub before assuming the car needs a wider or narrower setup. A bent mount or a displaced bumper can put a previously correct tire against a fender. Inspect it with power disconnected.

On dry dirt, check the wheel assemblies, links and transmission area after the session. The 272R has a documented parts breakdown, and the slipper adjustment and gear cover have their own locations. If the vehicle seems to lose drive, follow the exact inspection and status guidance. A faulty controller, loose wheel interface and slipping clutch can all feel similar through the trigger, so identify the system before replacing a part.

Keep the model code with your setup notes. The regular Slash offers many parts with similar names, while this car has oval-specific mounts, wheels and body details. Use the Late Model drawing to identify the assembly and the Fox entry when you need the exact appearance. A part that fits the chassis family may still change clearance or legality.

09 /

Other complete cars make sense on other tracks

Choose the Slash 2WD XL-5 if you want the conventional short-course layout and the exact brushed package fits your course or class. Look at its box contents and race rules separately. The late-model body and low chassis are the reasons to pay attention to the Fox car. A shared name will not make either stock setup a direct substitute for the other.

The Team Associated Pro2 SC10SW is a complete short-course truck for a driver who wants a different rear-drive chassis and the associated body and equipment. It is worth comparing when you run recreational short-course lines rather than a club’s late-model class. Read the exact package review to establish the startup gear and battery compatibility.

The Rustler 2WD XL-5 uses a stadium-truck shape with exposed wheels. Pick it if a low fendered body works against what you drive most often or if the primary goal is learning throttle control on ordinary recreational ground. On a prepared oval with a rule set built around the Late Model, buy the vehicle that meets the rule and fits the line.

10 /

Buy the oval car for the oval you actually have

The Fox Edition gives a dirt-oval driver a coherent place to start. The low chassis, enclosed shell, BL-2s electronics and 272R transmission are already installed. The setup adjustments will keep a careful driver interested, and the no-reverse profile addresses a specific race-day need. The value depends on being able to use those pieces at a track.

If you only have rough ground or if your club runs a different spec, move on to a car that fits those conditions. Check local class rules and power equipment before you price the car, and make a plan for your first practice session. The stock line on a familiar oval will teach you more about the purchase than a motor swap made before the first lap.

Confirm 104364-74-FOX, keep a record of the battery position and leave time for one small adjustment at a time. For an eligible racer who wants to buy a complete, assembled late model with its factory livery, this is a straightforward starting point.

BEFORE YOU ORDER /

The questions worth asking.

Is the Fox Edition a full RC car?+

Yes. 104364-74-FOX is the assembled vehicle with body, electronics and radio. The 10423-FOX replacement shell is a separate part.

Does it come with a battery?+

The drive battery, charger and four radio AAs are sold separately.

Does Race mode change the battery protection setting?+

Race mode changes the available drive commands by disabling reverse. Confirm the separate low-voltage protection setting for the battery chemistry using the manual.

Can I race it at any club?+

Check local rules for the exact chassis, motor, tires, battery and transponder. The manufacturer’s RTR designation does not establish local eligibility.

What gearing number should I use when checking a class rule?+

Use the installed pinion and spur and the manual’s method. The manual specifies a 2.72 internal factor. Traxxas’s website labels 9.10:1 as internal, so verify the installed gears and follow the manual’s calculation.

Can I run it on a rough lawn?+

The stock low chassis and fendered body are aimed at prepared dirt. A rough lawn can interfere with the shell and consume clearance.
