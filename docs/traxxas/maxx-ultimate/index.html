01 /

Pay for the equipment you can point to

Start with the body off. The blue Maxx Ultimate makes its case with steel CV shafts, belted tires, a metal-gear steering servo and the factory-installed Link module. Those are visible differences with consequences for setup and repair. The 540XL 2400Kv motor and VXL-4s controller remain in the 4S Maxx class. If the purchase needs to change what you can do with the truck, work through the hardware list and your venue before letting the trim name set your budget.

The exact blue manufacturer number is 89087-4-BLUE. The 89087-4 manual covers the shared Ultimate chassis, and the model also comes in other colors. A listing can shorten the model name to Maxx, which does little to separate it from the 89086-4 we reviewed earlier. Check the suffix, wheels, servo and parts sheet when comparing a seller listing, especially if you are looking at a truck advertised as upgraded.

The standard Maxx is the comparison to put on the other side of the bench. A buyer who expects to install comparable shafts, wheels and steering can price that actual shopping list and factor in their time. If your list consists of a battery and some afternoons in a field, the Ultimate premium is harder to make back in use.

03 /

A 4S system with substantial hardware around it

The 540XL is a sensorless brushless motor rated at 2400Kv. That rating describes motor speed per volt under its reference condition. Tire size, gearing, battery voltage under load and terrain all affect what the truck does on the ground. The VXL-4s controller and its motor should be considered together. Use the supported battery and gearing combinations in the exact manual instead of treating a physically fitting pinion or pack as a supported setup.

The center differential is part of how the front and rear axles share drive while allowing a speed difference. The front and rear gear differentials do a corresponding job between the wheels on each axle. Steel CV shafts connect that system to the corners, and the splined hub locates the stock wheel. The stronger factory components still have service points. A tight or dirty bearing, damaged boot or loose wheel nut deserves attention on its own.

Start with stock gearing and stock-diameter tires. A larger tire effectively asks the motor to move the truck farther for each turn of the wheel. Extra load also shows up on heavy grass, in repeated hard starts, and when something is binding. If the truck slows or displays a warning, use the ESC status instructions and inspect the car before driving again. More throttle is a poor way to diagnose increasing drag.

04 /

Set up the truck for your site before changing a setting

Start with the pack specified for the exact truck, the correct balance charger and fresh transmitter AAs. Read the Quick Start and the battery and radio sections before first power. Check that the pack sits properly in the tray, the latch captures it, and leads have room away from moving parts. Check wheel retention, tire condition and body seating. A truck this size benefits from those checks every time it comes out of the car.

The included TQi radio has the Link Wireless Module and supports Traxxas Stability Management. TSM provides steering assistance. Begin with the factory setup, establish trim on clear ground as directed, and then change only one variable at a time. If you want to compare a lower TSM setting, make two passes through the same turn at a steady speed and leave the throttle profile and tires alone for the comparison.

Use the available low-power driving profile according to the VXL-4s manual when getting comfortable with a smaller area. Pick a visible brake marker and watch where the truck stops before adding speed. Leave room beyond the marker, especially on loose dirt. The radio and software can help you tune the controls. The braking margin still comes from the course and where you decide to lift.

05 /

Why the 2085RL and belted tires belong in the same discussion

Traxxas pairs the 2085RL metal-gear servo with a double-shear aluminum horn and WideMaxx steering geometry. With a 405 mm wide truck and large, aggressive tires, steering hardware has real work to do, particularly when the tread is digging in on the spot. The fact that the servo is factory fitted matters for a buyer who wants the pieces designed to work together without sorting horn fit and linkage geometry.

The Sledgehammer tires have internal belting that limits expansion, and the factory wheel fits a keyed 17 mm splined hub. When replacing a wheel, read the hub, wheel offset and tire diameter together. A listing that only says 17 mm does not settle the shape of the drive interface or how much clearance you will have at full steering and suspension travel. Consult the Ultimate diagram and inspect the wheel when it goes on.

Leave the factory alignment as a reference. After a hit, check the servo saver, steering linkage and free movement with power disconnected before changing trim. With the vehicle operating safely according to the manual, verify it tracks straight at low speed. A drift caused by a bent link or a tire catching bodywork needs a physical correction. Adjusting the radio first can make the same fault harder to see.

06 /

Give the WideMaxx stance enough room to work

The Ultimate is 572 mm long, 405 mm wide and listed at 4.63 kg. Put those measurements next to the patch of ground you actually drive. Open dirt and short grass give the tires room to work and let you set up a turn well before the edge of the route. On a small lawn, a few strides of apparently clear ground disappear quickly once you account for corners, braking and a recovery lane.

The listed center clearance is 67 mm. That helps over uneven ground, but the ends of the truck and suspension still meet obstacles at their own heights and angles. Walk the route for roots, holes and stony transitions before driving. Begin with lower speeds, then repeat a line after it is clear you have grip and room to stop. Watch for ground that catches one wheel and can pitch a tall truck into a roll.

Use a wide, predictable run-in for a low jump only if you can see the landing and the runoff. Leave the rougher line for later if it cuts across your braking zone. On mixed surfaces, use the lowest-grip part of the route when planning the stop. This truck earns its place on a site that lets you drive a full line without having to spend the whole battery managing the boundary.

07 /

Service the actual Ultimate assembly

The reinforced clipless shell is a practical feature. It comes off for the battery, debris inspection and a closer look after a rollover. Check both the latch and the supports rather than judging the shell by its paint. A displaced mount can keep the shell from settling normally. With the battery disconnected, look for a pinched lead, dirt around the latch or an obvious crack before trying to close it again.

After a run, remove grass from the wheel areas and check the CV boots and wheel retention. Compare left and right suspension movement if a corner starts to sit lower. The aluminum shock bodies, blue links and heavy-duty steering do not erase normal wear. The useful ownership habit is to find a part number from the exact diagram, then verify whether it is shared with the regular Maxx before placing an order.

For a used truck, ask the seller to show the blue model code, both axles, the steering servo label, the wheel interfaces and the Link module. A standard truck can wear an Ultimate body. It is also possible for a real Ultimate to have replacement hardware that no longer matches the factory list. If there is a mismatch, use the price of the truck as it sits and budget any restoration you want. The included manual and diagrams give both buyers a useful reference for that conversation.

Pay particular attention to tires before a return to open ground. A change in vibration, visible damage or glue lifting at the rim deserves inspection and repair according to the manual. Replace with compatible assemblies so differences in diameter or fit do not become a new variable. Keep a small service log with the pack, surface, symptom and last change. It makes a parts conversation much easier than trying to recreate the issue from a short video.

10 /

Buy the whole configuration or keep the standard truck

The blue Maxx Ultimate makes sense when you already know how and where you will drive it. The wide footprint and tire choice suit rough recreational routes. The servo, shafts and radio are the pieces that distinguish the purchase. You can see exactly where the money went, and you have a model-specific parts list for putting the truck back to factory condition.

For the driver who wants a familiar 4S truck and will only take it onto uncomplicated ground, the standard Maxx deserves the first quote. The Ultimate only earns the added expense when its equipment is something you want on the car. If you are coming from a smaller truck, try describing the corner and braking zone you have available. The larger vehicle is a genuine change in where you can drive comfortably.

Check for 89087-4-BLUE before ordering, price a compatible power setup, and keep the wheel and driveline pages in reach. Spend the first session establishing a clean stop, a repeatable turn and a known baseline. Then decide which adjustments improve the truck where you drive. There is little reason to undo a carefully matched factory package until you can identify a problem it leaves unsolved.

BEFORE YOU ORDER /

The questions worth asking.

How is the Maxx Ultimate different from the standard Maxx?+

It adds factory equipment including steel CV shafts, belted Sledgehammers, 17 mm splined hubs, a 2085RL servo and the installed Link module. Both are in the 4S Maxx class.

Does the blue package come with a battery or charger?+

No. Budget a compatible drive pack, balance charger and four AA transmitter batteries.

What does the blue suffix identify?+

89087-4-BLUE identifies the exact blue factory Ultimate package. The shared manual uses 89087-4.

Can I fit any wheel described as 17 mm?+

Check the splined interface, wheel offset, tire diameter and actual clearance. The width designation alone is insufficient.

What is a useful first terrain?+

Open, firm dirt with a clear turn and braking area. Start on stock gearing and follow the setup instructions for the power equipment and radio.

Does TSM apply the brakes?+

It is steering assistance. The driver still chooses the speed, line and braking point.
